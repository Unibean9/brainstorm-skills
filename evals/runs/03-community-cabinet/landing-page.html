The friction

The tools are nearby. The trust is not.

Residents do not know whom to ask. Owners worry about damage, loss, and nobody being responsible. Community Cabinet makes the handoff visible.

For the borrower

Find the thing, not the person.

Start at the cabinet. See what is available and what action comes next, instead of broadcasting an awkward request.

For the lender

Lend with a record, not a hope.

Registration, custody events, condition history, and a clear damage rule make the next state legible.

The mechanism

A physical place. A visible loop.

The cabinet keeps the neighborhood grounded. QR codes connect each physical item to its current record; one steward keeps the two states aligned.

The human system

Trust has a job description.

The MVP names the roles that make custody real. It does not begin as an open marketplace or a citywide network.

Borrower

Use it. Return it.

Start custody, follow the return expectation, and report the observed condition.

Lender

Offer it with clarity.

Register the item, confirm its starting condition, and review its history.

Steward

Keep states honest.

Update availability, inspect returns, and route damage or loss through the rule.

Before launch

The honest parts are still open.

A clear rule is part of the MVP. Its exact responsibility, remedy, targets, and fallback details need the building committee's decision.

What is the Damage rule?

The pilot needs an approved rule for damaged items, including responsibility, review, and next action. The rule is not invented here.

What does “on time” mean?

The success signal is directional: items return on time. The expected-return definition and numeric target remain open.

What happens when a QR code cannot be scanned?

The physical Cabinet needs a non-scan fallback. The pilot must define it before launch.
