- [event][user turn 1] The basic idea is a shared cabinet of household tools for a neighborhood.
- [event][user turn 2] Every home has a drill, ladder, vacuum, or camping item that gets used only a few times a year.
- [event][user turn 3] People who need an item do not know whom to ask and feel awkward bothering a neighbor.
- [event][user turn 4] Owners worry about damage, loss, and nobody being responsible.
- [idea][user turn 5] An open marketplace app where people list items and message each other was considered.
- [idea][user turn 6] A smart locker in the building lobby was considered.
- [idea][user turn 7] A paper board, QR codes, and one neighborhood steward were considered.
- [idea][user turn 8] Renting tools from a local store and delivering them was considered.
- [question][user turn 9] The group asked how borrowing and returning could work if residents do not want to download an app.
- [question][user turn 10] The group asked which damage rule would make an owner willing to lend again.
- [question][user turn 11] The group asked which model can operate without a support team if only twenty households participate.
- [insight][user turn 12] An open marketplace would create too many messages and unclear availability.
- [insight][user turn 13] A smart locker is expensive and a power outage could block access.
- [insight][user turn 14] A paper board is cheap but someone must update availability and inspect returned items.
- [decision][user turn 15] The group chose a physical cabinet with QR codes and one neighborhood steward; it does not start as an open marketplace.
- [decision][user turn 16] The MVP includes item registration, borrow and return, condition history, and a clear damage rule.
- [decision][user turn 17] The first pilot is one neighborhood; the concept does not promise a citywide network.
- [direction][user turn 18] Success means lenders come back and items return on time; numeric targets remain open.
- [event][user turn 19] The handoff requests a complete PRD, one HTML landing page, and one deep HTML pitch deck using prd.md for the building committee.
- [override] Headless/replay mode is explicit; the 19 golden user turns are canonical and no new user claims are introduced.
- [assumption] Any named journey personas, visual treatment, and unconfirmed numeric thresholds are placeholders or open questions, not observed evidence.
- [decision] PRD is final and the explicit MVP boundary remains one neighborhood, physical cabinet, QR codes, one steward, item registration, borrow/return, condition history, and a clear damage rule.
